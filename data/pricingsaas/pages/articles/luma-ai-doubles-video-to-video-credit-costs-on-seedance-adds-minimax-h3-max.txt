WHY IT MATTERS
As AI video platforms add more models to their lineups, credit pricing for the most compute-intensive modes - like video-to-video, which re-renders existing footage rather than generating from a prompt - is becoming the lever vendors pull to protect margins without touching the cheaper, more-visible Text/Image-to-Video price points. Watch whether Luma's video-to-video costs keep oscillating as it adds further models, and whether other video-generation vendors start splitting price moves the same way: cuts on the headline modes, quieter increases on the heavy ones.

THEIR RATIONALE
Luma doesn't publish rationale for these model-cost tweaks, but the sequencing tells a story: this hike lands just 10 days after a roughly 50% cut to the same Video-to-Video costs, and under two weeks after a cut of up to 73% across the whole Seedance lineup. For a company that changes its pricing page every nine days on average, this reads less like a single pricing decision and more like active recalibration - pairing the increase with the debut of a new, more capable model suggests Luma is managing margins on compute-heavy video generation while leaving its headline Text/Image-to-Video numbers untouched.

THE SIGNAL
This is Luma's first price increase among its eight tracked pricing changes since July - the rest have been cuts, feature swaps, or new model launches. That makes it an outlier against Luma's own record, but not against the category: credit and usage-based repricing is the single most common type of pricing move in the index right now, touching 135 changes across 109 companies in the last six months. What stands out is the whiplash - cutting Video-to-Video costs twice in eleven days, then doubling them - which suggests Luma had underpriced the heavier video-to-video workload relative to compute cost and is now correcting, using the new model launch as cover for the increase.

IN CONTEXT
Luma AI's Video-to-Video credit hike is a reversal, not a continuation: it lands just 10 days after Seedance 2.0 costs were cut roughly 50%, and two weeks after a steeper cut of up to 73% across the Seedance lineup. Of Luma's eight tracked changes since tracking began in July, this is the first increase — the rest were cuts or additions like Seedance 2.5's debut. Text-to-Video and Image-to-Video pricing stayed untouched, aiming the hike squarely at the costlier video-to-video workload as the new Minimax H3 Max model joined the lineup. Credit and usage-based repricing is the category's most common move — 135 changes across 109 companies in six months — but Luma is now one of the few in that group raising video-to-video costs rather than cutting them.

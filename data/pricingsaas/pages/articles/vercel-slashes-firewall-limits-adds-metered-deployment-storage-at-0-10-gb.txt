Vercel slashes Firewall limits, adds metered Deployment Storage at $0.10/GB
Vercel cut Web Application Firewall limits across tiers: Custom Firewall Rules on Enterprise fell from 1,000 to 250; IP Blocking rose on Hobby (3 to 10) but fell on Pro (100 to 40) and Enterprise (1,000 to 250); System Bypass Rules fell on Pro (25 to 10) and Enterprise (100 to 75).

WHY IT MATTERS
Deployment Storage adds another metered primitive alongside sandboxes, Connect and Drives — all monetized within the same year of near-weekly pricing edits — so buyers should expect the metered surface area to keep expanding. Enterprise teams evaluating Vercel's security tier should budget for continued downward pressure on included limits rather than treat this cut as a one-off. Watch whether the 'Beta' relabel on Daemons becomes a template — bill first, backfill the disclaimer after — on whatever primitive Vercel meters next.

THEIR RATIONALE
Vercel hasn't framed this as a correction, and the page carries no sale or promo language — it reads as a straightforward capacity cleanup released alongside a new revenue line. Trimming Enterprise's highest-value security caps the same day a metered Deployment Storage add-on goes live suggests Vercel is tightening included entitlements on one side of the ledger while opening a new metered one on the other, consistent with its steady cadence of granular, usage-based repricing — 24 real changes in the last 12 months, about one every 11 days.

THE SIGNAL
The sharper story isn't the raw caps — it's the order of operations on Daemons. Vercel turned on $0.128/hr Active CPU billing for Daemons, then applied a Beta label afterward, the reverse of the usual beta-then-GA-then-billing sequence other vendors follow when a feature graduates. That inversion matters more on a security-adjacent line than a feature flag: Enterprise customers who sized workflows around a 1,000-rule firewall and IP-blocking ceiling now have a quarter of the headroom, with no transition window called out on the page. Beta tagging is also a narrow pattern across the tracked index — only 2% of changes in the last 180 days use it — so reaching for it on an already-billed feature reads less like a maturity signal and more like a hedge.

IN CONTEXT
Vercel has touched pricing roughly every 11 days over the past year, and this is its eighth move into credits-and-usage billing — coming six days after Vercel Drives storage launched at $0.05/GB-month, itself close on the heels of Daemons picking up $0.128/hr Active CPU billing on Oct 16. That same Daemons feature is now relabeled 'Beta,' inverting the usual order where a feature ships in beta and graduates once billing starts. The cuts land hardest on Enterprise, where Custom Firewall Rules and IP Blocking caps both drop from 1,000 to 250, and System Bypass Rules falls from 100 to 75. Beta labeling itself is rare across the index — only 2% of tracked changes in the last 180 days touch it — but Vercel is already tied with Figma and Notion as the pattern's second-most-frequent user, behind only HubSpot.

PACKAGING CHANGE
GitLab brings Secrets Manager to Self-Managed, Dedicated still pending
GitLab's Secrets Manager, a centralized service for storing, controlling access to and rotating credentials, was marked "New" and available for Premium and Ultimate plans.
See before and after

GitLab’s pricing page · Change Detected Oct 16, 2026
WHY IT MATTERS
Self-Managed customers, often in regulated or air-gapped environments, now get native credential management without waiting on GitLab.com feature parity to trickle down — one less reason to run a third-party secrets vault alongside GitLab. But Dedicated accounts, typically GitLab's highest-compliance customers, are the ones most likely to need this and are the ones still waiting. Watch whether Dedicated gets Secrets Manager within GitLab's usual ~52-day change cadence, or whether it lags further behind.

THEIR RATIONALE
GitLab is rolling Secrets Manager out deployment tier by deployment tier rather than shipping it everywhere at once — GitLab.com first, Self-Managed next, Dedicated still to come. That staged approach matches the company's recent cadence: it moved just 28 days after renaming Flex per-use credits to "on-demand" and dropping overage discounts, faster than its typical 52-day gap between changes, and follows a year of incremental platform additions like adding Claude Code and Codex to Duo Agent Platform in March.

THE SIGNAL
This is a capability-parity move more than a packaging one: GitLab isn't changing what Secrets Manager costs or who can buy it, it's closing the gap between what GitLab.com customers get and what self-hosted customers get. Feature-level changes like this are GitLab's single most common type of pricing-page move — 9 of its 24 tracked events — and a routine category across the whole index, accounting for about 11.7% of the 1,720 pricing changes tracked in the last six months. The more telling fact is what's still missing: Dedicated, the tier aimed at GitLab's largest and most regulated customers, doesn't have it yet.

IN CONTEXT
Secrets Manager first appeared on GitLab's pricing page as a 'New' card limited to GitLab.com; this update extends it to Self-Managed and leaves Dedicated as the remaining gap. GitLab has made 10 real pricing-page changes since tracking began in April 2025, and this one landed just 28 days after it renamed Flex per-use credits to on-demand and dropped overage discounts — faster than its typical 52-day cadence.

WHY IT MATTERS
Buyers comparing Fin Voice should not treat $1.99 per outcome as Intercom's list price yet: many visitors never see it, and Intercom's own documentation still points to contract pricing. Watch whether the redesigned page wins the test and becomes permanent, which would make Intercom one of the first support vendors to publish a per-outcome voice AI price with a money-back guarantee.

THEIR RATIONALE
Intercom hasn't announced the redesign or the $1.99 price. Its help center still says Fin Voice is available to select customers through sales, with pricing set in each customer's order form. Testing a page that publishes a per-outcome voice price, a money-back guarantee and spending caps suggests Intercom is measuring whether up-front price and risk protection lift conversion before it commits to them publicly.

THE SIGNAL
This is Intercom's second live pricing experiment at once. It is also testing its Essential seat at $19 against $29, which runs independently of this layout test. Running two experiments in parallel points to a company actively searching for its AI-agent pricing rather than settling on it.

IN CONTEXT
Intercom has been repricing at an unusually fast clip: this is its sixth change in 90 days against a typical ~60-day gap, coming just eight days after it ended the $19 Essential promo and pushed seat price back to $29, which itself followed a 34% Essential price cut three weeks earlier. Layering a 90-day money-back guarantee and spending alerts onto Fin Voice AI reads as Intercom building trust into usage-based AI pricing rather than just chasing adoption. That instinct is shared broadly: feature-addition changes like this one accounted for 271 of 1,462 tracked moves in the last 180 days, with Vercel, Together and Mintlify adding most often, though none paired their additions with a guarantee this explicit.

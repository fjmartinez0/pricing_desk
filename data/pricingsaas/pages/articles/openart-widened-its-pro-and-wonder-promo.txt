DISCOUNT CHANGE
OpenArt widened its Pro and Wonder promo
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 10, 2026
OpenArt widened its premium model promo to up to 50% off.
See before and after
Enlarge
Openart’s pricing page · Change detected Nov 10, 2026
What changed
Pulse detected the change on Nov 10, 2026: OpenArt's promo banner on its pricing page now offers up to 50% off three models — Kling 4.0, GPT Image 2.6 and Recraft V4.1 — for Pro and Wonder plan subscribers. The banner had offered just 30% off GPT Image 2.6 generations since OpenArt added that promo on Nov 5, 2026, according to its pricing page. Plan prices on Starter, Plus, Pro and Wonder are unchanged from before.
Why it matters
OpenArt leans on rotating, time-boxed promo banners rather than list-price cuts to drive upgrades: Pulse tracked 26 pricing changes for OpenArt in the last 365 days, about one every 7 days on average, and its previous discount change landed just 5 days earlier, on Nov 5, 2026. For a product leader running usage-based AI pricing, the move is a reminder that a single promo banner can carry real weight: moving from one discounted model to three, at up to 50% off instead of 30% as of Nov 10, 2026 per OpenArt's pricing page, meaningfully changes the effective price of running premium generations on Pro and Wonder without touching its published seat prices. Watch whether OpenArt's next change widens the promo further, lets it lapse on schedule, or folds the discount into a permanent tier change.
In context
Pulse has tracked six discount-banner changes for OpenArt since it began tracking the company on May 16, 2026 OpenArt, most recently with a 30% off GPT Image 2.6 offer on Nov 5 — the habit reads less like a one-off and more like a lever OpenArt pulls roughly weekly. Pulse tracked 118 of 3,065 pricing changes across the companies it tracks in the last 180 days as discount changes — 3.8% — with Upstage (9) and Kodekloud (7) changing theirs most often. Peers ran the same seasonal playbook this week: Hostinger deepened its Black Friday deal and Format swapped its annual promo for a Cyber November cut. The sharper read: widening a discount's scope — from one model to three — rather than just its size is a cheaper, easier-to-reverse lever than repricing published seat prices, and a sign OpenArt is still testing willingness-to-pay on its newest models.
Common questions
What would this actually cost or save a Pro subscriber using one of the newly discounted models?
OpenArt's banner caps the discount at “up to 50% off,” per OpenArt's pricing page, which usually means the rate varies by model — multiply your typical monthly spend on that model by the rate shown at checkout, since the banner gives no flat dollar figure.
Should we run time-boxed model discounts like this instead of changing list prices?
It's a lower-commitment lever than repricing a plan — a promo banner can be narrowed or dropped the next time Pulse checks the page without touching published seat prices, worth weighing if you want to test demand for a new model without disturbing your price ladder.

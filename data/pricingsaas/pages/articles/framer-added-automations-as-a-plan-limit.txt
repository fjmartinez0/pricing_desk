Framer added Automations as a plan limit
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 25, 2026
Framer turned Automations into a limited, plan-based feature.

What changed
Framer's plan comparison table now caps Automations by tier: Free gets 1, Basic 3, Pro 10, and Enterprise a custom limit set on request. The cap shows up as a new Automations row directly below CMS items in the table; the rest of the page, including plan descriptions and sign-up buttons, is unchanged.
Why it matters
For a product leader, Automations arrived on the pricing page as a counted, tiered limit from day one, the same way Framer already meters CMS items, static files and AI credits. Capping it by tier gives Framer a new upgrade lever — a team that outgrows Basic's 3 automations has a concrete reason to move to Pro's 10, or negotiate Enterprise's custom ceiling.
Teams building their own packaging should watch whether this newly countable metric becomes something Framer references in future pricing moves, and whether Free's cap of 1 automation is generous enough to keep self-serve signups from feeling limited on day one.
In context
Pulse has tracked 18 pricing changes from Framer since it began following the company on Mar 23, 2026, averaging one every 14 days, including a Nov 24, 2026 change that quintupled free-plan bandwidth to 5 GB the day before this one. Per Pulse's tracked history, Framer's static-file limit on Pro jumped tenfold, from 5 to 50 files, on Sep 24, 2026 more, one of several limit changes Framer has made this year. Feature-gating moves like this are common: Pulse tracked feature additions in 951 of the 3,864 pricing changes across 657 companies it logged in the last 180 days, with Together and MindsDB moving most often. What the raw diff doesn't show is the pace: this is Framer's second pricing change in two days, a sign the company is iterating on packaging rather than settling on a stable tier structure.
Common questions
Does the Automations cap scale with team size?
No — the limit is per plan tier, not per seat, so a larger team on Pro still gets 10 automations total regardless of headcount; teams that need more have to upgrade to Enterprise's custom limit or consolidate workflows into fewer automations.
Is this a one-off limit or part of a larger pattern?
Part of a pattern: Framer has added or tightened limits on CMS items, static files and AI credits over the past few months, and this is its second pricing change in two days — expect more metering of features that used to feel unlimited.
Should we cap our own early-stage automation features the way Framer did?
If adoption is still low, a generous free cap (Framer's is 1) costs little and still creates an upgrade path; the bigger signal here is pairing the cap with a visible table row so customers see the limit before they hit it.

Fireflies narrowed its Enterprise HIPAA claim to require a BAA
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS

Fireflies narrowed its Enterprise plan’s HIPAA claim to require a signed BAA.

What changed
Fireflies relabeled the Enterprise plan’s compliance line from “HIPAA compliance” to “HIPAA-ready (Enterprise, with BAA),” per Fireflies’ pricing page on Nov 10, 2026. The new wording ties the HIPAA claim to Enterprise customers who have signed a Business Associate Agreement (BAA), rather than stating compliance as a blanket plan feature. Prices are unchanged on all plans.
Why it matters
This is a precision move on a compliance claim, not a feature or price change — but it matters for anyone running security reviews or RFPs: an unqualified “HIPAA compliance” line on a pricing page invites exactly the scrutiny Fireflies appears to be heading off by tying the claim to a specific instrument (a signed BAA) and a specific plan. If your own pricing page states a compliance certification as a blanket plan feature rather than a conditional one, this is a prompt to check whether your wording would hold up the same way under a buyer’s legal review. Watch whether Fireflies also revisits the FERPA language it added to the same line on Oct 22, 2026, now that the underlying HIPAA wording has changed.
In context
Fireflies tightened its Enterprise HIPAA wording just 19 days after it last touched the same line: on Oct 22, 2026 it added ISO 42001 AI certification and expanded HIPAA to cover FERPA Oct 22, 2026, and Pulse has tracked seven changes to Fireflies’ pricing in the last 365 days, about one every 27 days. No earlier Fireflies change carries Pulse’s “positioning changed” tag, so this rewrite of its own compliance wording is a new kind of edit for the company, not a repeat. The shift fits a wider pattern of companies sharpening their own claims: Pulse tracked 276 positioning changes across 233 companies in the last 180 days, 9% of the 3,065 pricing changes it recorded, led by OpenAI. Basecamp made a similar wording change on Nov 9, 2026, renaming a group-chat feature to Chats & Pings with prices unchanged Nov 9, 2026. The sharper read: a compliance claim written as a plan feature is a liability until it’s written as a condition — and Fireflies just rewrote its own to match.
Common questions
Is the FERPA language Fireflies added on Oct 22, 2026 still there?
Looks that way — the pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 10, 2026 only edits the HIPAA wording and adds the signed-BAA condition; nothing in that line points to FERPA being touched this time.
What should this mean for security due diligence on Fireflies?
Ask for the actual signed BAA rather than relying on the plan-card label — Fireflies’ own wording change shows that a bare “HIPAA compliance” checkbox item can be rewritten overnight, so the document itself is what actually holds up.

PACKAGING CHANGE
GitLab cut the included GitLab Credits on Premium and Ultimate
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 20, 2026
GitLab cut the GitLab Credits bundled with Premium and Ultimate.

What changed
GitLab cut the promotional GitLab Credits bundled into its paid plans: Premium's included allowance drops from $12 to $8 per user per month, and Ultimate's from $24 to $16, according to the pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 20, 2026. These credits are what let Premium and Ultimate subscribers use GitLab's AI features without buying the separate GitLab Credits add-on. Subscribers already on Premium or Ultimate keep their current, higher credit amount through Dec 31, 2026 — the lower allowance applies to new and renewing subscribers going forward.
Why it matters
For a product leader selling AI features behind a credits meter, this is a low-drama way to tighten unit economics without touching the number that gets screenshotted on the pricing page: subscription prices hold, while the included AI allowance that comes bundled with them shrinks on both Premium and Ultimate. It's also a soft nudge toward GitLab's separate Credits add-on and other credit-consuming products — a smaller bundled allowance means users burn through it faster and are more likely to buy top-ups. The grandfather clause here (existing subscribers keep today's higher amount through Dec 31, 2026) is worth copying for your own cuts: it buys goodwill and gives customers a deadline to notice, rather than a silent reduction they discover mid-invoice. Watch whether GitLab trims the allowance again once that grandfather period ends — a second cut right after the first would turn this from a one-time trim into an established pattern.
In context
GitLab has been reworking its credits system all year, per Pulse's record: it rebranded Flex's per-use credits to on-demand and dropped overage discounts in September 2026 the Flex rename, launched unified seat-and-credit billing back in June Flex Commitment, and priced a new $12,000/year Advanced Capacity add-on just 20 days before this cut, on Oct 31, 2026 the Advanced Capacity add-on. That is five changes in the last 90 days against GitLab's own roughly 42-day average gap, per Pulse's record, a faster pace than its own history suggests is normal. Credits and usage billing are a live category well beyond GitLab, too: Pulse tracked 416 pricing changes across 276 companies touching credits or usage billing in the last 180 days, 12.2% of the 3,414 changes tracked overall. For a peer team, the sharper read is that GitLab is tuning the AI-credits meter in both directions, pricing a steep new high-capacity add-on while trimming the free allowance that ships with the base plans, rather than settling on one model.
Common questions
Does this affect what we currently pay for our subscription?
No — Premium and Ultimate list prices aren't changing; only the bundled AI-credit allowance shrinks, so your invoice stays the same unless you're buying extra GitLab Credits to cover the gap.
What should we check before our grandfather period ends?
Mark Dec 31, 2026 on your calendar if you're a current GitLab subscriber — the item doesn't describe a renewal notice, so confirm with GitLab directly rather than assuming the lower allowance won't apply until you're told otherwise.
Is this cut part of a broader shift at GitLab?
It looks that way: GitLab has made three other credits-related changes this year, including a new high-capacity add-on priced about three weeks before this cut, which suggests an active re-tuning of usage pricing rather than a one-off adjustment.

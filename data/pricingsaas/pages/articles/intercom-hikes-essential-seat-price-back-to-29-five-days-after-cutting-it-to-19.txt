Intercom is running a pricing test: Essential shows $19 to some visitors and $29 to others
Our October 3 capture shows Essential at $29/seat/mo on annual billing, up from the $19 our September 28 capture recorded, with Advanced ($85) and Expert ($132) unchanged.

WHY IT MATTERS
The $19 price is real for the visitors who draw it, so anyone who signed up at $19 should check whether their annual contract locks that rate or resets to $29 at renewal. For buyers, a reload in a fresh browser may show the other price. For the category, a pricing page can be a running experiment rather than a fixed decision, and reversals within days are the tell. Watch which arm Intercom keeps: a $19 standing price would be a 34% cut on the entry seat, while $29 means the test ends quietly.

THEIR RATIONALE
Intercom hasn't stated a reason, and it doesn't need to: the price is an experiment. On October 5 we loaded the pricing page in eight fresh browser sessions at the same minute. Two showed Essential at $19 and six at $29, with Advanced and Expert identical in every session. The page source names the test, team-self-serve-essential-price-reduction, alongside a second experiment on Pro pricing and a layout test on the page itself, and assigns visitors on first visit. Our daily capture lands in one arm each time, which is why the price appears to flip: September 28 was a $19 session, October 3 a $29 one.

THE SIGNAL
Essential's seat price has now touched $29, $19, $29, $19 and $29 within five weeks, more churn on one line item than Intercom showed on seat pricing in its prior two years of tracking (seven seat-pricing moves since per-seat billing launched in January 2024). Read as an experiment, that churn is one question being asked repeatedly: does a $19 entry seat convert enough extra teams to beat $29? Seat-price moves are common across the index, 57 companies made 66 of them in the last 180 days, but a controlled test with both prices live is the first we have caught in the act.

IN CONTEXT
Intercom cut the Essential seat price 34% to $19 on September 28, then five days later reversed it, pushing the price back up 52.6% to $29 — undoing its own cut. That's the third move on this exact price point in five weeks: the $29 standard had already resurfaced once, when a 35% new-customer promo lapsed on August 31. Intercom has touched seat pricing seven times since adopting per-seat billing in January 2024, but reversing a cut this fast is new — its typical gap between changes runs 63 days. Seat-price churn is common: 57 companies made 66 such moves in 180 days, including Beehiiv's seat cut and Cognition's seat cap. Rarer is an outright increase — just 3.9% of tracked changes raised price, which is what makes this about-face notable.

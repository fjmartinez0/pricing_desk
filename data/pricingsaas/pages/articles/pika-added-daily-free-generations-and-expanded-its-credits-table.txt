Pika added daily free generations and expanded its credits table
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 4, 2026
Pika added free daily H3 Flex generations for its paid plans.

What changed
Pika's Creator and Fancy plans now include a limited-time perk of free daily Pika H3 Flex generations, called out on both plan cards and in a summary note below the pricing grid, per the pricing-page update Pulse saved on Nov 4, 2026.
Per Pulse's Nov 4, 2026 record of the page, the credits table grew from 4 AI models to 14, adding Pika H3 Flex, Pika 2.5, more Seedance and Kling variants, Seedream 5 Lite, Ideogram 4.5, ElevenLabs v4 and MiniMax Music 3.0. Per that same Pulse record, the Free plan's card tagline also switched from stating a credit count to marketing copy.
Why it matters
For a product leader watching usage-based billing, here's what this page says: Pika is willing to give away a frontier model's output for free on paid tiers rather than cut price, and it's broadening its credits table faster than it's raising what credits can buy. Pulse tracked 324 pricing changes touching credits-and-usage billing across 224 companies in the last 180 days — 12.1% of the 2,688 changes it tracked in that window — so expanding a model catalog without touching price is a well-worn move right now, not a Pika-specific experiment.
If your own packaging runs a credits-per-model table, the read is that model count itself has become a selling point: a bigger catalog signals more capability per credit without having to defend a new price. Watch whether the free H3 Flex perk survives past its limited-time window — if Pika turns it into a standing plan benefit, that's the clearer signal that giving away capacity is replacing price cuts as this market's go-to lever.
In context
Pika has seven events on Pulse's record since Jun 8, 2026, six of them real pricing changes, and this change lands nine days after Pika's Oct 26, 2026 move to add a commercial license to the $10 Starter plan and launch a plan recommender (Pika's Oct 26 update) — faster than its own roughly 28-day average gap between changes. Pulse hasn't logged a free-plan change on Pika's page before now, so the daily-generations perk is a first for this pattern on Pika: market-wide, Pulse tracked 170 free-plan changes across 142 companies in the last 180 days, 6.3% of the 2,688 it tracked, with Wisprflow logging the most at five. The credits-table growth fits a bigger wave — per Pulse, Synthesia dropped its credit calculator on Nov 3, 2026, and Seedance doubled its free starter credits on Nov 2, 2026, the same week Pulse logged Pika's update. Pika isn't cutting price here, it's widening what a credit buys and giving away capacity instead — a cheaper lever than a discount, and easier to quietly end.
Common questions
Is the daily free H3 Flex perk likely to stick around, or should we expect it to end?
The diff flags it as a limited-time perk on the plan cards themselves, which is Pika's own signal, not Pulse's inference — budget for it to disappear rather than treat it as a durable price cut, and recheck Pika's pricing page once the window named there runs out.
If we wanted to try a similar free-generations perk, how would we size the giveaway?
Pulse's diff doesn't record the daily cap Pika set for H3 Flex generations, so there's no number here to copy — check Pika's pricing page for the actual limit and treat it as a compute cost per active subscriber per day, not a standard rate to assume.
Could a giveaway like this cannibalize revenue from our own credit packs or top-ups?
Pulse's record doesn't show Pika's credit-pack or top-up revenue, so there's no way to size that risk from this diff — the safer move if you try something similar is to time-box it with an explicit 'limited time' label the way Pika did, so buyers read it as a promotion rather than a renegotiated unit price they'll expect to keep.

Snowflake swapped Snowpark callouts for AI agents and compliance claims
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 5, 2026
Snowflake rewrote its plan callouts to tout AI agents and compliance instead of Snowpark.

Snowflake’s pricing page · Change detected Nov 5, 2026
What changed
Snowflake rewrote the feature callouts on its Standard, Enterprise and Business Critical pricing tiers. The earlier copy highlighted Snowpark, multi-cluster compute and Tri-Secret Secure encryption; the new copy drops those and instead promotes AI agents (Snowflake’s CoWork and CoCo features), AI search, and compliance coverage for HIPAA, PCI DSS and FedRAMP.
Pulse detected the change on Nov 5, 2026. It comes 87 days after Snowflake’s previous pricing-page change, which Pulse tracked on Aug 10, 2026, when the company hid its default pricing behind a platform and region selector.
Why it matters
This is a positioning change, not a packaging change — Snowflake didn’t touch prices, tiers or limits, only the language selling them. But what a vendor chooses to lead with on its pricing page signals where it thinks the budget conversation is moving: swapping Snowpark and multi-cluster compute (infrastructure capability) for AI agents and AI search (outcome-oriented capability) lines up with a broader shift from seat- and compute-based pricing toward AI-native framing across SaaS.
For a product leader, the lesson isn’t the specific features — it’s the move itself: if your own pricing page still leads with infrastructure or technical specs, a rewrite toward what the AI features do for the buyer may land better with the same plans and the same price points, no repricing required. Pulse found feature-callout rewrites like this in 299 of the 2,691 pricing changes it tracked across 250 companies in the last 180 days — 11.1% of all changes — so the pattern is common but not yet dominant.
Watch whether Snowflake follows the copy change with an actual pricing or packaging move for its AI agent features in the next few months — a rewritten callout is often the precursor to a priced tier.
In context
Snowflake has made 4 real pricing changes (5 events) since Pulse began tracking it on Jan 1, 2024, most recently on Aug 10, 2026, when it hid default pricing behind a platform and region selector [[snowflake/2026W33]]. This is the first of those changes to touch feature callouts rather than price or trial terms — the two before it were an Apr 27, 2026 removal of free-trial messaging [[snowflake/2026W18]] and a 2024 storage price cut [[snowflake/2024Q2]]. Pulse tracked 299 feature-callout rewrites across 250 companies in the last 180 days, led by HubSpot [[hubspot]] and Perplexity [[perplexity]] with six each and Modal [[modal]] with four, so Snowflake is following a well-worn pattern, not setting one. The sharper read: trading Snowpark and multi-cluster compute for AI agents and AI search is Snowflake narrating its platform as an AI product, not a data warehouse — the same reframing Figma [[figma]] and OpenAI [[openai]] have made in their own plan copy — and it costs nothing in price or limits to test that story before committing to a priced tier.
Common questions
Does this change what a Snowflake customer actually pays?
Not on its own — but a feature-callout rewrite like this is often how a vendor previews what it will package or gate next, so it is worth checking this page again in a few months to see whether the AI agent features move behind a paywall or usage meter.
Should we rewrite our own pricing page to lead with AI rather than infrastructure?
Only if your AI features are far enough along to carry the claim — Pulse’s data shows this kind of callout rewrite is already common (11.1% of changes in the last 180 days), so it reads as catching up, not standing out.
Does Snowflake say why it made the swap?
No — the pricing page carries no rationale copy for the change, so there’s no quote to cite; the earlier move it made, hiding default pricing behind a region selector on Aug 10, 2026, also shipped without an explanation.

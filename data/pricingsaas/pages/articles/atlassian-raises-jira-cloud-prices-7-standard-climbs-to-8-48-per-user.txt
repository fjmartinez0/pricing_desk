PRICE INCREASE
Atlassian raises Jira Cloud ~7%: Standard $8.48, Premium $15.56 per user at 300 seats
Between the Oct 13 and Oct 14 captures, Jira Cloud's per-user monthly prices at the page's default 300-user team size rose about 7%: Standard from $7.91 to $8.48 and Premium from $14.54 to $15.56.

WHY IT MATTERS
Teams on monthly Jira Standard or Premium billing at this size pay about 7% more per user, roughly $171 a month more for 300 Standard users and $306 more for 300 Premium users. The pricing page itself carries no notice of the change, so it's worth flagging to whoever owns the Jira budget before the next invoice.

THEIR RATIONALE
The pricing page carries no announcement or promotional framing for the increase, and its copy, feature lists and CTAs are otherwise untouched, so this reads as a list-price update on the Standard and Premium tiers rather than a repositioning. It is the third Jira price increase Pulse has on record: two earlier increases took Standard from $7.16 to $7.53 and then to $7.91, and Premium from $12.48 to $13.53 and then to $14.54.

THE SIGNAL
Standard rises $0.57 and Premium $1.02 per user per month, which adds about $171 and $306 a month for a 300-user team. It also places Atlassian inside a live industry pattern: seat pricing touched 95 of 1,735 tracked pricing changes (5.5%) across 79 companies in the last six months, including Intercom's promo rollback and Katalon's move to annual-only per-seat billing.

IN CONTEXT
Pulse has recorded 9 earlier changes on Atlassian's Jira pricing page since November 2023, most recently an automation-quota overhaul on Sep 7 that tightened per-plan caps. This increase lands in a six-month stretch where seat pricing touched 95 of 1,735 tracked changes across 79 companies, from Intercom ending its Essential seat promo to Katalon moving Professional to annual-only per-seat pricing. Other recent seat moves took different shapes: Replit added SCIM and audit logs to Enterprise, Figma bundled a new Specs beta into its seat tiers, and Cartesia dropped its unlimited-seat promise — a reminder that seat-pricing activity spans increases, bundling and promo cleanup, not just one direction.

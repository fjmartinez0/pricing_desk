Framer dropped its Content editors seat tier

Framer removed its $10 Content editors seat tier.

What changed
As of the Nov 28, 2026 change Pulse detected, Framer's pricing page no longer lists the Content editors seat tier, which had cost $10 per editor per month on Basic and Pro and a custom rate on Enterprise. The $20-per-editor Additional editors tier is now the only paid way to add collaborators, per Framer's pricing page as of Nov 28, 2026.
Why it matters
For teams that need more than one person touching content without full design access, this removes a cheaper path to that access: with Content editors gone, Basic and Pro teams must use the pricier Additional editors tier for any content-only collaborator. For a product leader, it's a reminder that narrow, cheaper seat tiers aimed at lightweight collaborators tend to get folded into the standard seat price once they stop being a growth lever — a seat lineup that looks generous in a beta or launch phase often simplifies down later. Watch whether Framer replaces the gap with a different low-cost seat, or whether this is pricing simplification ahead of a broader change to its seat lineup.
In context
Pulse has tracked Framer since Mar 23, 2026 and logged 19 real pricing changes in that span, including 9 in the last 90 days — averaging roughly one every 14 days — so this lands just 3 days after Framer added Automations limits to Free and Pro on Nov 25, 2026. Framer has touched seat pricing before too: Pulse recorded Framer raising Basic's seat limit from 2 to 10 users in June 2026. Seat pricing is a busy lever market-wide: Pulse tracked seat-pricing moves in 205 of 3,880 pricing changes across 146 companies in the last 180 days, with Invideo and Intercom among the most frequent. The sharper read: cutting the cheap Content editors tier pushes Framer's seat structure toward fewer, pricier buckets rather than a graduated ladder, three days after it added Automations as a new plan limit.
Common questions
Should we worry our own entry-level collaborator seat could get cut too?
There's no stated reason here, so don't read this as an industry signal — but it's a cue to check whether your own cheap, narrow-scope seat tier is still being promoted or quietly phased out the way Framer's Content editors tier was.
Does this change what Enterprise customers pay for content-only editors?
The removed tier had a custom (non-fixed) rate on Enterprise rather than a flat per-editor price, and neither the item nor Framer's pricing page says whether Enterprise lost a parallel option — worth confirming directly if it affects your contract.
How do we know this isn't a temporary promo or A/B test?
The pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 28, 2026 shows the Content editors row removed outright, with no promo badge, discount countdown, or other test marker — the signals that usually flag a live experiment — so this reads as a straightforward packaging change, not a reversible test.

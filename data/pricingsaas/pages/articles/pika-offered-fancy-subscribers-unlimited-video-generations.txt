Pika offered Fancy subscribers unlimited video generations
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 18, 2026
Pika gave Fancy subscribers unlimited Wan 3.0 video generations at no extra cost.

What changed
According to Pika's pricing page, as Pulse saved it on Nov 18, 2026, Pika gave Fancy subscribers unlimited generations with its Wan 3.0 video model this week, instead of the usual metered-credit billing. According to Pika's pricing page, as Pulse saved it on Nov 18, 2026, the promo caps each generation at up to 10s at 720p, one at a time, and ends Nov 23, after which credit metering resumes.
Why it matters
For a product leader watching usage-based AI pricing, this costs Pika nothing on its price list: it is temporarily lifting the usage cap on one video model instead of cutting a price, pulling demand toward Wan 3.0 without asking Fancy subscribers to learn a new number. Pulse tracked nine changes to Pika's credit and usage pricing since Jun 8, 2026, the pattern this promo extends with one more short-lived variable in a credit system Fancy subscribers already have to re-learn often.
For teams running their own credit-based pricing, a time-boxed ‘unlimited’ window is a lighter lever than a price cut: it borrows urgency from a deadline rather than asking subscribers to rationalize a new rate, and it reverses itself automatically once the promo expires. Watch whether Wan 3.0 keeps a more generous credit rate after the promo ends on Nov 23, according to Pika's pricing page — that would mean the free week was a demand test, not just a perk.
In context
Pulse tracked 13 pricing changes to Pika since Jun 8, 2026, averaging one every 13 days [[pika]]. This promo arrives the day after Pika repriced GPT Image 2.6 and Nano Banana 2.1 by up to 33%, per Pulse's Nov 17, 2026 record. Pulse tracked nine changes to Pika's credit and usage pricing since Jun 8, 2026, including cutting Starter and Creator credit caps and adding Nano Banana 2 at 7 credits — frequent, granular tuning rather than one big repricing. Pulse tracked 400 credits-and-usage-billing changes among 3,298 pricing moves in the last 180 days, 12.1% of the total, with Lumaai and Invideo the next most active after Pika [[pika]]. The sharper read: a free week on one model is a low-cost way for Pika to test demand before deciding whether Wan 3.0 earns its own credit rate — the same kind of experimentation showing up in Invideo's recent credit rework elsewhere in the market.
Common questions
How would this affect a Fancy subscriber who regularly maxes out their Wan 3.0 credits?
For this week, their usage isn't metered at all — multiply their typical per-clip credit cost by expected volume to see what they're temporarily not spending, then watch whether that usage data shapes a permanent Wan 3.0 rate once credits resume.
What happens to Wan 3.0 billing after Nov 23?
Standard metered-credit billing resumes for Wan 3.0, according to Pika's pricing page; Pika hasn't said whether a lower permanent rate follows, which is the detail worth watching.
Is a time-boxed ‘unlimited’ promo like this worth copying?
It works best for a single usage-metered feature you want to pump adoption of without reopening your price list — set a hard end date up front, as Pika did, so subscribers don't need to parse a policy change.

PACKAGING CHANGE
GitLab launches Orbit add-on, a context graph sold as query packs
GitLab added a new "GitLab Orbit" card to its pricing page, described as a context graph for the software lifecycle, sold in query packs sized to the organization that form one shared pool for humans and agents.

WHY IT MATTERS
Orbit tests whether GitLab's credit-based billing, built for usage-based AI features, extends cleanly to a context-graph product sold in query packs rather than per seat. Watch whether the Self-Managed and Dedicated launch in early November reveals concrete query-pack pricing, and whether more of GitLab's add-ons migrate into the credits system instead of carrying their own flat price.

THEIR RATIONALE
GitLab frames Orbit as part of the broader GitLab Credits system it has been building out for its usage-based AI features, rather than as a standalone priced SKU. That fits its recent direction: repositioning Duo Agent Platform for offline license deployments, streamlining add-ons pricing into a unified layout, launching Flex Commitment's unified seat-and-credit billing, and renaming per-use credits to on-demand while dropping overage discounts. Orbit is sold through that same credits mechanism rather than introduced as a new flat-rate add-on.

THE SIGNAL
Across the index, new add-ons made up 125 of the 2,350 pricing changes tracked in the last 180 days across 108 companies, led by Vercel and Baseten — and Orbit is GitLab's first tracked entry in that pattern. That stands out against GitLab's own record: of its 25 tracked pricing events since Apr 11, 2025, feature changes are the most common type (9), far more than any kind of new launch. GitLab has mostly repriced, repositioned or restructured what it already sells rather than adding net-new paid add-ons, which makes Orbit a break from form.

IN CONTEXT
GitLab has logged 25 tracked pricing events since Apr 11, 2025, and nearly all of them have reshaped what it already sells — feature changes account for 9 of them, more than any other type. Orbit breaks that pattern: it is GitLab's first tracked new-add-on launch, arriving 14 days after Secrets Manager reached Self-Managed, with Dedicated still pending, and it rides the same credits system GitLab built out with Flex Commitment's unified seat-and-credit billing in June and September's rename of per-use credits to on-demand. Across the index, new add-ons made up 125 of the 2,350 pricing changes tracked in the last 180 days across 108 companies — a pattern GitLab is only now joining.

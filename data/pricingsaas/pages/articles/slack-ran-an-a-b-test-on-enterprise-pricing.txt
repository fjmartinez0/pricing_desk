What changed
According to Slack's pricing page, Enterprise+ listed a public rate of $45 USD per user per month, billed annually, on the version Pulse saved on Nov 3, 2026. The version Pulse saved on Nov 8, 2026 shows that price removed, replaced by "Contact sales for pricing."
A $45 price, according to Slack's pricing page, and "Contact sales for pricing" have alternated on the Enterprise+ listing since the version Pulse saved on Oct 4, 2026 first showed the public rate. The plan name, description and feature list are unchanged across every version Pulse has saved.
Why it matters
For product leaders using Slack's top-tier pricing as a reference point, an unstable public number is not a reliable anchor right now. According to Slack's pricing page, Enterprise+ carried a $45 per-seat rate on the version Pulse saved on Nov 3, 2026, and "Contact sales for pricing" again on the version Pulse saved on Nov 8, 2026 — which points to a live test rather than a finished pricing decision. Teams citing $45, per Slack's pricing page saved Nov 3, 2026, as the company's enterprise rate should treat it as one tested value, not a committed price, until the page settles on one state.
It is also a reminder that a single snapshot of a competitor's pricing page can catch either arm of a live test. Pulling a number from one visit, instead of checking the page across repeated visits, risks baking a figure into a deck or model that the company itself hasn't settled on.
Watch whether Slack's Enterprise+ page holds steady on one state over the next few weeks — that would mark the end of the test and the number worth citing going forward.
In context
According to Slack's pricing page, Enterprise+ carried a $45-per-seat rate on the version Pulse saved on Nov 3, 2026, and "Contact sales for pricing" appeared on the version Pulse saved on Oct 13, 2026 and again on Nov 8, 2026 — a faster flip than Slack's 63-day average gap between pricing changes, per Pulse's tracking since Jun 2023. Repeated same-day fetches of Slack's pricing page found the $45 price, per Slack's pricing page, on only about half the checks on Nov 8, 2026, the signature of a live test rather than a settled reprice. Pulse tracked 147 seat-pricing changes across 108 companies in the last 180 days, with Intercom and Figma among the most frequent movers — but those were committed changes, which makes Slack's back-and-forth the outlier here, not the pattern. The sharper read: Enterprise+ may not have a committed public price yet, so a $45 quote, per the version of Slack's pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 3, 2026, risks citing the losing arm of an unfinished test, not Slack's settled rate.
Common questions
How can I tell if Slack's Enterprise+ price has settled?
Save a fresh copy of Slack's pricing page yourself and compare it against the version Pulse saved on Nov 8, 2026 — if the page still shows "Contact sales" a week or two later, the test has likely resolved against the public price.
Is this part of a wider shift away from public enterprise pricing?
It doesn't look like it elsewhere — most companies Pulse tracks that touched seat pricing in the last 180 days made a single committed change, not a repeated flip, so this looks closer to a one-off experiment on Slack's page than a tactic to copy.
What should I do before citing Slack's Enterprise+ price in a comparison?
Re-check Slack's pricing page yourself and note the date you saved it — the listed price flipped in just five days this time, far faster than Slack's typical 63-day gap between pricing changes, per Pulse's tracking since Jun 2023.

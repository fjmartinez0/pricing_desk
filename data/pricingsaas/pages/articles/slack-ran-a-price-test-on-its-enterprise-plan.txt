Slack ran a price test on its Enterprise+ plan
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 28, 2026
Slack is running a pricing-visibility test on its Enterprise+ plan page.
See before and after

What changed
Slack's Enterprise+ plan page showed a public price of $45 per user/month, billed annually, on the pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 28, 2026, replacing the "Contact sales for pricing" listing. Pulse had already shown the same price on the Slack pricing page it saved on Nov 25, 2026, three days earlier.
A fetch of Slack's pricing page on Nov 28, 2026 found a third-party experimentation script active on every one of 8 fresh page loads, with the price appearing on 6 of those loads and "Contact sales for pricing" on the rest. That split, a price on some loads and not others, points to a live pricing test rather than a settled repricing.
Why it matters
If this is a live pricing test, Slack has not actually settled on whether to show Enterprise+ pricing in public; it is measuring whether a visible number changes who books a sales call. Teams sizing Enterprise+ should treat the $45-per-seat figure Pulse saw on Slack's pricing page on Nov 28, 2026 as provisional, not a confirmed list price, until it holds across repeat visits.
For other companies gating enterprise pricing behind "Contact sales," this is a cheap way to learn whether a published number helps or hurts the pipeline before committing to it: worth copying if price is a known objection, risky if sales reps can't explain why the number keeps moving.
Watch whether the $45-per-seat price on the page Pulse saved on Nov 28, 2026 holds on the pages Pulse saves next, or whether Slack reverts to "Contact sales" once the test ends.
In context
Pulse has tracked Slack's Enterprise+ price flipping between a shown figure and "Contact sales for pricing" repeatedly since Oct 4, 2026, with reveals on Nov 3, Nov 14 and Nov 25 and a hide on Nov 8, before today's Nov 28 reveal. That pace is far faster than Slack's own average of one change every 50 days, per Pulse's tracking since Jun 1, 2023. Pulse tracked 205 seat-pricing changes across the companies it tracks in the last 180 days, so a move on seat price is not itself unusual — flipping the same number on and off this many times recently is. A third-party experimentation script now runs on Slack's own pricing page, confirmed by Pulse's fetch on Nov 28, 2026; the sharper read is a live test of whether a visible Enterprise+ price helps or hurts the sales pipeline, not a settled decision. A reveal that reverts within days says more about which test arm a visitor lands in than about Slack's actual price.
Common questions
Does this mean Slack has permanently revealed Enterprise+ pricing?
No. Check Slack's pricing page yourself before quoting the figure to a prospect or an exec: since the page is still serving more than one version, your own browser could land on either one until the test settles.
How should we estimate what Enterprise+ would cost our own team at this rate?
Multiply the per-seat annual rate by your seat count to get a monthly estimate, if the price Pulse saw on Nov 28, 2026 holds. Slack's sales team would still need to confirm it for a deal of any size, especially while the page keeps flipping between that price and "Contact sales."
Why would Slack test whether to show a price at all?
Hiding enterprise pricing is a common way to protect a sales team's room to negotiate. Testing a visible number lets Slack see whether a concrete figure changes who starts or abandons a sales conversation before it commits either way.

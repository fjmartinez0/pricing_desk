Slack launches a Frontline teams add-on for Enterprise+ field workers
Slack added a new "Frontline teams" add-on to its Enterprise+ plan, giving field workers access to task management, agentic support, and secure Salesforce integration.

WHY IT MATTERS
The launch signals Slack is willing to carve out a separately priced layer of access for non-desk, frontline workers rather than folding that reach into its existing per-seat plans, as collaboration platforms look for seats beyond the office. Watch whether Frontline teams gets a disclosed price of its own — Enterprise+ itself is in a live test between a $45 price and Contact sales — or stays locked behind "Contact sales," and whether Slack extends similar field-worker access to its other plans.

THEIR RATIONALE
The add-on extends Slack's collaboration platform to frontline, deskless workers — often a harder segment for collaboration software to reach since they are not always at a desk — by pairing task management with secure Salesforce access and its existing agentic AI tooling. The add-on lands just six days after Slack's last pricing-page change on Oct 14, 2026, part of an unusually active stretch: Slack has made 4 pricing changes in the last 90 days, a faster pace than its roughly 75-day average gap between changes.

THE SIGNAL
This is only Slack's third new paid add-on since Pulse began tracking it in June 2023, after the Atlas directory tool (Jan 2024) and the Enterprise Grid AI add-on (Apr 2024) — a pattern Slack uses rarely, and is now reviving on the same tier, Enterprise+, where Pulse has tracked a live A/B test since Oct 4, with some visitors seeing a $45 price and others seeing Contact sales. Across the index, new add-ons are a modest but real pattern: 101 of the 1,995 pricing changes tracked over the last 180 days (5.1%) involved a new add-on, spread across 88 companies, with Baseten, Gusto and Stripe the most frequent. Slack isn't chasing that volume — it's using the add-on model to open a new paid surface for a worker segment its core per-seat plans don't typically reach.

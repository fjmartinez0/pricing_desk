PACKAGING CHANGE
Captions renamed its Enterprise plan to Tesseract for business
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 5, 2026
Captions renamed its Enterprise tier to Tesseract for business.

What changed
Captions renamed its Enterprise tier to "Tesseract for business." The pricing page no longer shows the "Enterprise" or "Custom pricing" labels; it now reads "Making videos at scale? Talk to our sales team about Tesseract for business," with the same Contact sales button as before.
The itemized benefits list that used to sit under the Enterprise card is also gone — bulk credit discounts, custom seat options, a dedicated account management team, training data exclusion, customer onboarding and priority training, and early access to beta features are no longer spelled out on the page.
Why it matters
For a product leader, this is a packaging move, not a pricing one: Captions didn't touch what Max or Frontier cost, but it pulled back what "Enterprise" visibly includes. A prospect evaluating Captions at scale can no longer self-qualify by reading the page — they now have to start a sales conversation before learning whether bulk credit discounts, dedicated account management, or training-data exclusion are even on the table.
That's a common move when a vendor wants to negotiate enterprise terms deal-by-deal rather than defend a published list, but it also adds friction for self-serve-minded buyers who'd rather compare line items before picking up the phone. Teams weighing their own enterprise tier should watch whether Captions brings any of those perks back once "Tesseract for business" gets its own dedicated page, or whether the vaguer framing sticks.
In context
Pulse has tracked Captions's pricing page since Jul 6, 2025, logging 38 comparisons and 6 real changes in that span, spaced about 79 days apart on average. Pulse recorded the last change 44 days earlier, also a rename — Scale became Frontier on Sep 22, 2026, without touching price — but this is the first time Captions has paired a rename with pulling benefits off the page entirely. Pulse recorded the original build-out on Mar 30, 2026 — adding Enterprise as a custom tier with a published benefits list — meant to make custom pricing legible; this diff undoes some of that by swapping the itemized list for a one-line pitch to "talk to our sales team." The sharper read: Captions is leaning further into negotiated, sales-assisted enterprise deals rather than a self-serve comparison — a plausible move for any vendor tired of publishing a list prospects then negotiate against, though Pulse has recorded only two renames for Captions since Jul 6, 2025, so it's too early to call this a settled pattern rather than a one-off cleanup.
Common questions
Did Captions's enterprise pricing actually change?
No — this is a copy and packaging change, not a price move; nothing on Max or Frontier moved either. If you're sizing a deal, you'll now need to ask Captions directly which of the old perks survive, since the page no longer lists them.
Is this part of a broader pattern for Captions?
The real tell will come later: watch whether Captions publishes a dedicated page for Tesseract for business with its own feature list, or whether "contact sales" becomes the permanent default for every future Enterprise-tier update.
What should we check before copying a move like this?
Before swapping a published "what's included" list for a "contact sales" banner, map out which specific line items your own sales team would need to defend one-on-one — a bulk-discount or data-exclusion promise that disappears from the page doesn't disappear from what prospects expect once they're on a call.

PACKAGING CHANGE
Docker launched Cloud Sandboxes and trimmed its Desktop plan cards
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 20, 2026
Docker launched Cloud Sandboxes, pay-as-you-go compute priced at $0.07 an hour.

CLOUD SANDBOXES·first seen Sep 28, 2026
What changed
Docker rebuilt its pricing page around product lines instead of a single plan ladder. New Cloud Sandboxes pay-as-you-go pricing is now published, running from $0.07/hr for the Micro size up to $1.12/hr for XL, according to the pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 20, 2026. The page also added Sandboxes (a free, local isolated environment for coding agents) and AI Governance (custom-priced, centrally managed network, filesystem and MCP policy controls). Docker Desktop's plan cards changed from Personal, Pro, Team and Business to Free, Teams and Custom: Pro now appears only in the comparison table, and the old Business card became a Custom/Contact-sales tier. Subscription prices for the plans that remain listed did not move, according to the same page Pulse saved on Nov 20, 2026: Pro is still $9, Team $15, and Business $24 per user per month on annual billing.
Why it matters
For a product leader whose own pricing page tries to serve both self-serve developers and enterprise buyers, Docker's move is a reminder that restructuring around product lines (containers tooling vs. cloud compute vs. governance) can simplify the page even as it adds a brand-new usage-based line item. Collapsing the card lineup removes a decision point for most buyers, but it also demotes Pro to a comparison-table footnote and turns Business into a straight contact-sales tier — a trade worth watching if your own funnel depends on a visible mid-tier card. The new Cloud Sandboxes line puts Docker squarely in the pay-as-you-go compute business, not just container tooling, which is a different sales motion and a different metric to watch than a flat per-seat subscription. Watch whether Docker's existing Pro and Team subscribers get nudged toward Sandboxes usage billing over time, or whether it stays a separate, optional line.
In context
Docker has been building up to this for weeks: Pulse's record shows a $250 Cloud Sandboxes credit promo added Sep 28, 2026 in place of a whitepaper CTA the September promo, pulled Oct 27 the Oct 27 pull, and put back the next day, 23 days before this rebuild the Oct 28 relaunch. That back-and-forth, plus an Oct 12, 2026 move dropping Build Cloud minutes from the Pro, Team and Business listings the Oct 12 change, reads like a company testing messaging before committing to the structural rebuild shipped here. It is also a slower mover than most: Docker's own page has changed roughly every 80 days on average since Jan 2024, per Pulse's record, well behind the pace Pulse sees elsewhere for feature additions — 824 changes across 592 companies added a feature in the last 180 days, 24.1% of the 3,414 tracked. For a peer team, the lesson is that a page this overdue for a redesign tends to arrive all at once — a new product line, a trimmed plan ladder and a reworked architecture together, not piecemeal.
Common questions
Does this change what we'd pay on Pro, Team or Business?
Check the comparison table, not the card row, if you're pricing out Pro going forward — Docker did not drop the plan, it just stopped giving it a dedicated card, so a quick glance at the page could make you think it disappeared.
How would Cloud Sandboxes pricing work for a typical workload?
Multiply the hourly rate for whichever Sandbox size you use — Micro at the low end, XL at the high end — by your expected runtime hours; the item describes no included allowance, so budget it as pure pay-as-you-go compute.
Is the free Sandboxes product the same thing as Cloud Sandboxes?
They're easy to conflate by name alone, so check which one a given workload actually uses: Sandboxes needs nothing beyond a free, local setup, while Cloud Sandboxes starts billing by the hour the moment you provision one.

PACKAGING CHANGE
Krea raised the cost of Nano Banana generations
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 7, 2026
Krea raised Nano Banana 2.1's per-image generation cost.
See before and after
Enlarge
Krea’s pricing page · Change detected Nov 7, 2026
What changed
Nano Banana 2.1 (1K) image generation now costs about 25 units per image, up from about 22, cutting Krea's advertised per-plan estimates roughly 11% across the board — Basic from about 225 to 200 generations, Pro from about 900 to 803, Max from about 1,800 to 1,607, and Business from about 900 to 803 — per the pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 7, 2026.
Separately, Krea's plan-selector cards switched from quoting Nano Banana 2 to Nano Banana 2.1 — a model swap, not added capacity — with Basic's listed estimate moving from about 112 to 200 generations, Pro from 450 to about 803, Max from 1,350 to about 2,410, and Business from 1,800 to about 3,214, per the same pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 7, 2026. Unit allotments and dollar prices are unchanged, and so are the Nano Banana 2 estimates themselves.
Why it matters
For a product leader running usage-based AI pricing, this is a reminder that the real price of a plan lives in the units-to-output exchange rate, not just the sticker price or quota number on a comparison page. Krea didn't touch its dollar prices or monthly unit allotments — only the per-image cost of one model moved — but that's a lever that's easy to miss when benchmarking a competitor off a screenshot of their pricing page.
The plan-card swap compounds the risk: a card that now quotes a richer-sounding generation estimate for Nano Banana 2.1 than it used to show for Nano Banana 2 looks like added capacity, but it's really a different model's math. Any team quoting competitor capacity numbers in a sales deck or board update should confirm which model generation those numbers describe before repeating them.
Watch whether Krea applies the same per-unit repricing to its other models next, or whether this stays isolated to Nano Banana 2.1.
In context
Per Pulse's tracking, Krea has leaned on limit cuts before, trimming MiniMax H3 Max video quotas 4x on Sep 17, 2026, roughly halving them on Sep 1, 2026, and slashing Flux Kontext allowances on Aug 27, 2026 — a company more comfortable shrinking what a unit buys than raising sticker prices. This move follows the same playbook on the unit-to-output exchange rate itself, five days after Krea trimmed free-plan LoRA training, per Pulse's record, on Nov 2, 2026. Pulse tracked 147 pricing changes involving limit cuts across 131 companies in the last 180 days — about 5.4% of all changes tracked — so quiet repricing like this is a recognizable pattern among the companies Pulse tracks, not a Krea-specific anomaly. The sharper read: because Krea repriced the model's cost basis rather than the plan's advertised unit total, a reader comparing plan cards over time would see numbers move in confusing directions depending on which model's estimate they're reading.
Common questions
How often does Krea reprice like this?
Krea has made 16 pricing changes in the last 365 days, averaging about one every nine days, per Pulse's tracking.
Where else does this repricing show up on Krea's page?
The same shift appears in Krea's “Compare Plans in Detail” table, separate from the plan-selector cards, per Krea's pricing page.
Should we consider the same lever for our own usage-based pricing?
If your own per-output cost is drifting up because an upstream model got pricier, moving the unit-to-output ratio is one option worth weighing against cutting published allotments outright — it reads as less drastic to subscribers even though the effect is similar.

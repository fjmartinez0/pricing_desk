PACKAGING CHANGE
Otter stopped listing per-tier custom AI meeting template limits
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 7, 2026
Otter removed the per-tier quota row from its meeting template comparison table.
See before and after
Enlarge
Otter’s pricing page · Change detected Nov 7, 2026
What changed
Otter removed the "Custom AI Meeting Templates usage limits" row from its plan comparison table. Per Otter's pricing page as it stood before Pulse detected the change on Nov 7, 2026, that row capped custom AI meeting templates at 5 meetings per user on Basic, 15 on Pro, 30 on Business, and unlimited on Enterprise. The row sat directly beneath the "AI Meeting Templates" feature line, which still lists Standard access on Basic and Advanced access on Pro, Business and Enterprise — that designation did not change.
What changed is narrower than it looks: Otter kept the same Standard/Advanced split for AI Meeting Templates itself, but stopped publishing the per-tier custom-template limits. A buyer comparing plans today no longer sees a specific meeting-template number anywhere on the page — just "Standard" or "Advanced."
Why it matters
For a product leader, the interesting part isn't that a number moved — it's that Otter stopped publishing one. Dropping a quota from a comparison table removes a concrete reason to evaluate tiers side by side: a buyer sizing a rollout can no longer tell, from the page alone, whether Business gives meaningfully more custom-template headroom than Pro. That ambiguity can push self-serve buyers toward a sales conversation instead of a self-directed upgrade.
It also changes what you can verify going forward. With the row gone, a change to the per-tier limit would no longer show up as a line-item change on Otter's pricing page. Teams that priced their own usage-based or per-seat caps around a published competitor number should note that "no published limit" is not the same as "no limit."
Pulse tracked 131 pricing changes across 120 companies that removed a disclosed feature or limit in the last 180 days — 4.8% of all 2,705 changes tracked in that window — so unpublishing a limit is an established, if still minority, packaging move, not unique to Otter. Watch whether Otter's in-product limits change now that they are no longer on the page.
In context
Otter's pricing page changes infrequently: Pulse has tracked 13 real pricing changes on it since Jan 2024, and its most recent earlier move, on Jul 13, 2026, only narrowed eligibility for a Business-plan discount Business-promo narrowing. Other vendors have removed disclosed lines recently too: Postman cut SDK auto-distribution from Enterprise on Nov 4, 2026, and Lunar pulled a travel-insurance row from its plan cards on Nov 2, 2026. Pulse counted 131 feature-removal changes across 120 companies in the last 180 days — 4.8% of all changes tracked in that window — a real but still minority pattern. The sharper read: removing a published quota makes any later change to it harder for buyers to spot.
Common questions
How do we find out the real per-tier limit now that it's off the page?
Otter's page no longer states it; the previously published per-tier numbers are no longer guaranteed current, so confirm today's cap with Otter's team or by testing it in-product before sizing a plan.
Could our own pricing page have the same kind of quietly-dropped quota?
Audit your own comparison pages for quotas that exist in your database but aren't shown on the page — if a number isn't published, nothing on the page itself stops it from changing quietly, which is the risk this move highlights.
Is this connected to Otter's Business-promo change back in July?
Not evidently connected — the July move narrowed who qualified for a Business discount, a pricing lever; this change only touches what the comparison table discloses about an entitlement that itself hasn't changed.

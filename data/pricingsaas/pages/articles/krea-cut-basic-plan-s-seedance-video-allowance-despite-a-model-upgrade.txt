PACKAGING CHANGE
Krea cut Basic plan's Seedance video allowance despite a model upgrade
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 23, 2026
Krea cut Basic plan's monthly Seedance video allowance nearly in half.
See before and after
Enlarge
Krea’s pricing page · Change detected Nov 23, 2026
What changed
Krea's Basic plan dropped from about 31 Seedance 2.0 Fast videos to 16 Seedance 2.5 videos a month, per Krea's pricing page as of Nov 23, 2026 — a cut of roughly half even though the underlying model moved up a generation. The plan's price and its other listed allowances are unchanged; only the video quota line and the model version moved.
Why it matters
For a product leader, this is a reminder that a model upgrade is not automatically a value upgrade: Krea kept Basic's other terms unchanged while cutting the usable volume of its highest-profile new feature, effectively raising the per-video cost even as the underlying model got better. Teams that promise "upgraded for free" need to watch the denominator, not just the model name — a customer doing high-volume video generation on Basic will feel this cut directly, while a lighter user may not notice. It also fits a broader pattern at Krea: six of the 25 pricing-page events Pulse has tracked for Krea since Jun 15, 2026 were capacity cuts, alongside discount and freemium changes, so pricing and packaging here move often and in small increments rather than through one big repricing. Watch whether Krea backfills the quota on higher tiers or uses the freed capacity to justify a future price increase.
In context
Pulse has tracked five capacity cuts to a Krea plan since Aug 17, 2026, most recently trimming Nano Banana 2.1 estimates about 11% on Nov 7, 2026 [[krea/2026D311]], after cutting MiniMax H3 Max video limits 4x on Sep 17, 2026 [[krea/2026D260]]. This Seedance cut lands four days after Krea's Black Friday discount blitz of Nov 19, 2026 [[krea/2026D323]], in keeping with the roughly 8-day average gap Pulse measures across Krea's pricing changes since Jun 15, 2026. Pulse tracked 196 limit-cut changes across 164 companies in the last 180 days — 5.2% of the 3,741 pricing changes it tracked in that window — and Krea [[krea]] is itself a repeat offender within that group, not a one-off. The sharper read: pairing a capacity cut with a model upgrade softens the optics of a downgrade — the headline becomes "new model," not "less usage" — and understates how much pricier high-volume video generation on Basic just became. Watch whether Krea's other tiers see the same Seedance 2.5 quota cut, or whether Basic absorbs it alone.
Common questions
How much faster will a heavy Seedance user burn through the new Basic quota?
Anyone generating close to Krea's old Basic cap of about 31 Seedance 2.0 Fast videos a month, per its pricing page as of Nov 23, 2026, will run out roughly twice as fast under the new 16-video limit; the practical move is upgrading to a higher Krea tier or spacing Seedance renders across the billing cycle.
Should we price our own usage-based features the same way, bundling a model upgrade with a quota cut?
Expect a customer base generating near the previous cap to notice and push back, since "upgraded quality" reads as a downgrade when fewer renders come with it; a cleaner move is to hold the quota steady on an upgrade and cut it separately, with its own explanation, so the two signals don't blur together.
Does this capacity cut show up on Krea's other plans too?
The item Pulse flagged is scoped to the Basic plan's Seedance line; Pulse's diff for this change does not show the same cut on Pro, Max or Business, so for now it reads as Basic-only — worth checking back if Krea follows the same move up-market.

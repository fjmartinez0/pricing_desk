PACKAGING CHANGE
Bolt added collaborator limits to its Free, Pro and Teams plans.
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 17, 2026
Bolt began publishing a collaborator limit on its Free plan.

Bolt’s pricing page · Change detected Nov 17, 2026
What changed
Bolt's pricing page listed collaborator limits for the first time on Nov 17, 2026, according to Pulse: the Free plan is now capped at 3 collaborators, while Pro and Teams both show unlimited collaborators. The new line sits among the existing plan bullets; plan names, prices and the rest of each plan's features are unchanged.
Why it matters
For a product leader, this is a classic free-tier gate: capping collaborators at 3 (not seats sold) pushes multi-person teams toward a paid plan without touching price. Making Pro and Teams unlimited removes any seat-count friction once a team upgrades, so the cap's only job is to separate solo users from teams, not to meter usage by headcount. Teams weighing a similar move should check whether their own free tier already implies unlimited collaboration — introducing any numeric cap, even a generous one, creates the first visible wall a user can hit. Watch whether Bolt backs this with messaging that frames the cap as a collaboration feature rather than a takeaway, since a limit introduced without explanation can read as a restriction to existing free users.
In context
Pulse has tracked 11 pricing changes on Bolt since Jun 1, 2026, and had not logged a seat or collaborator limit on its page before this one; its previous change, a Pro feature swap, landed on Nov 9, 2026, eight days before this one Nov 9. Seat-style limits are common elsewhere: Pulse tracked 169 of 3,196 pricing changes in the last 180 days touching seat pricing across 123 companies, with Intercom the most frequent mover in that span. Most of those moves add or reprice seats rather than lift a cap, which makes Bolt's unlimited grant to Pro and Teams the less typical direction — Captions took the opposite path on Nov 10, 2026, naming exact seat counts instead: 2 on Max, 3 on Frontier. The sharper read: Bolt is using collaborator count, not seat price, as its free-to-paid lever, leaving its own prices untouched while Pika and others reach for per-seat pricing instead.
Common questions
Should we add a similar collaborator cap to our own free plan?
If your free tier currently allows unlimited people on a project, a small cap like Bolt's is worth piloting on new sign-ups only, not retrofitted onto existing users who could feel blindsided by a sudden limit.
What would push Bolt to raise or drop this cap later?
The record gives no stated reason for the cap, but Bolt has walked back limited-time offers before, including a free Forge promo on Pro that it later ended — so a cap introduced without explanation is a reasonable candidate for change if it hurts sign-ups.
Does the new cap apply to collaborators already on a Free project?
Bolt's pricing page doesn't say; it states the limit going forward, not a grandfathering rule for existing projects, so a team already past 3 collaborators should check directly with Bolt.

Vercel's trace retention extension is one arm of a live A/B test
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 6, 2026
Vercel extended how long it retains trace data.

What changed
Vercel raised Observability's trace retention across every plan — Hobby from 1 hour to 3 days, Pro from 1 day to 14 days (a 14x increase) and Enterprise from 3 days to 14 days — according to the pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 6, 2026. The comparison-table row that carries this limit was also renamed, from Session Tracing to Trace Retention, in the same update.
Pulse links this retention lift to an active A/B test on Vercel plan limits that has run since Oct 26, 2026 (first seen alongside Firewall rule-cap reductions on 2026D299), so the extended retention may be one arm of an experiment rather than a settled packaging change. The other arm Pulse has captured is reduced Firewall rule caps.
Why it matters
For product leaders shipping observability or logging features, this reads as a packaging move more than a price move — but only if it sticks: Vercel widened how far back customers can see trace history without touching the sticker price on any tier. A team debugging an incident that surfaces days later would have meaningfully more history on Pro, at no extra cost.
Because Pulse tags this as one arm of a live A/B test (Firewall rule caps reduced vs trace retention extended), treat the retention numbers as provisional until the experiment settles: a prospect who screenshots today's page may see the older 1-hour / 1-day windows on another visit. Watch whether Vercel keeps the longer retention in both arms, pairs it with usage-based pricing on trace storage the way it already prices Drains Volume and Daemons, or rolls the table back once the test concludes.
In context
Pulse has tracked 26 real pricing changes from Vercel since Dec 29, 2025, averaging one every 11 days, and this lands 4 days after its Nov 2, 2026 move to add usage pricing for Beta CI and Observability monitors [[vercel/2026W45]]. Pulse now tags both the Oct 26, 2026 Firewall rule-cap reductions [[vercel/2026D299]] and this Nov 6, 2026 trace-retention lift as arms of the same active A/B test on plan limits. It has made one other limit increase in that time, lifting Pro's Concurrent Sandboxes cap from 2K to 10K on Aug 27, 2026 [[vercel/2026D239]]. It is a market-wide pattern too: Pulse tracked 179 limit-increase moves across 151 companies in the last 180 days, 6.6% of the 2,701 changes it tracked in that window. The open question: whether the longer Trace Retention windows stay once the test concludes, or flip back.
Common questions
Does Pulse's record show why Vercel made this change now?
No — Vercel hasn't published a rationale Pulse can quote for this specific change, and nothing in its recent changes explains the choice of 14 days over a longer or shorter window; treat it as a product decision with no stated reasoning until Vercel says otherwise.
If I monitor Vercel's pricing page myself, what should I watch for?
Watch for silent label swaps, not just number changes: a text-match monitor keyed to the old row name would miss this update entirely, since the limit's row changed names in the same release, not just its value.
Could Vercel start charging for retention beyond 14 days?
Nothing in this change prices the extra days, but Vercel already meters related Observability add-ons by usage, according to Pulse's record: Deployment Storage at $0.10/GB since Oct 26, 2026 and Daemons at $0.128 per hour of Active CPU since Oct 16, 2026 — a metered extension here is worth watching for, not assuming.

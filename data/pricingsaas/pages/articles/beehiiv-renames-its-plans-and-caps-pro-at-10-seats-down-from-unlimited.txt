PACKAGING CHANGE
Beehiiv renames its plans and caps Pro at 10 seats, down from unlimited
Beehiiv renamed its three self-serve plans — Launch, Scale and Max — to Free, Lite and Pro; Enterprise pricing is unchanged.

WHY IT MATTERS
A hard 10-seat ceiling on Pro pushes larger newsletter teams toward Enterprise/custom pricing, which is where Beehiiv can capture more expansion revenue than an uncapped self-serve plan ever would. Given the company's accelerating cadence — four changes in 90 days, this one arriving right after a feature-removal pair — watch whether the next move doubles down on Enterprise monetization or whether seat headroom reappears if self-serve conversion takes a hit.
BY THE NUMBERS
Pro seat cap: unlimited → 10; Lite seat cap: 3 → 2
4th pricing change in 90 days; average cadence ~30 days
Second change in 4 days, after the Sep 28 NewsletterXP removal
Seat-pricing pattern touched 57 companies, 66 changes in the last 180 days
THEIR RATIONALE
There's no public messaging attached to the change, but the pattern reads as a self-serve clean-up rather than a cosmetic rebrand: this is Beehiiv's second change in four days and its fourth in 90 days, well above its roughly 30-day average cadence. Renaming to the generic Free/Lite/Pro ladder simplifies plan comparison, while capping Pro's seats tightens the economics of the plan most likely to host growing teams.

THE SIGNAL
The headline undersells it: an unlimited seat allowance on a $95-96/mo self-serve tier was unusually generous, and Beehiiv is now the newest entrant in a seat-pricing pattern that touched 66 changes across 57 companies in the last six months. Several of those moves landed the same week — Intercom cut its Essential seat price, Bugherd doubled seats while cutting price — but most of that activity sweetens entry tiers. Beehiiv instead tightened its top self-serve tier, trading a point of differentiation (unlimited seats) for a harder line between self-serve and Enterprise.

IN CONTEXT
Beehiiv's seat cut lands just four days after it dropped the NewsletterXP course from Max and Enterprise Sep 28's course removal, part of a run that's now four pricing changes in 90 days — well above its roughly 30-day average. Capping the self-serve Pro tier at 10 seats is also the company's first entry in the market's seat-pricing pattern, which touched 66 changes across 57 companies in the last six months, including Intercom's Essential seat-price cut and Bugherd doubling seats while cutting price — both the same week. Where peers mostly used seats to sweeten entry tiers, Beehiiv moved the opposite direction at the top, trading a generous unlimited allowance for a hard ceiling that should nudge larger teams toward Enterprise.

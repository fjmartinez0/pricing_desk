Pika cut Nano Banana 2's image cost from 8 to 7 credits
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 7, 2026
Pika added Nano Banana 2 to its credit table at 7 credits per image.

What changed
Pika added a new Nano Banana 2 row to its credit cost table at 7 credits per image, sitting alongside Nano Banana 2.1 at 5 credits, per Pika's pricing page as saved by Pulse on Nov 7, 2026. The model's per-image rate had been estimated at 8 credits before; with it now listed at 7, the same six top-up packs yield more images at unchanged prices, per Pika's pricing page on Nov 7, 2026: the $25 pack now returns 214 images instead of 187, the $50 pack 428 instead of 375, the $100 pack 857 instead of 750, the $200 pack 1.7k instead of 1.5k, the $500 pack 4.3k instead of 3.8k, and the $1,000 pack 9.4k instead of 8.3k.
Why it matters
For a product leader who prices their own AI output, this is a model-cost move, not a plan-price move: Pika lowered the credit cost of one specific model without touching subscription prices, which raises the value of existing top-up and subscription spend for anyone using that model. If your own pricing is metered per generation or per credit, that's the lever worth noting — when an underlying model gets cheaper, you can route the saving into what a credit buys rather than treating it as pure margin.
It also follows a credit-cap cut on Pika's Starter and Creator plans, according to Pulse's tracked history for Pika — a reminder that "credits" can move in different directions within the same pricing system depending on which lever Pika is adjusting. Watch whether Pika lowers per-image or per-clip rates on other models, or keeps adjusting the credit table while leaving headline plan prices untouched.
In context
Pulse has tracked Pika since Jun 8, 2026 and counted 8 real pricing changes, 7 of them in the last 90 days — most recently a Starter and Creator credit-cap cut on Nov 6, 2026 and a daily free H3 Flex generation add on Nov 4, 2026. Pika is also one of the most active companies in Pulse's credits-and-usage-billing lens, where 328 of 2,705 tracked changes (12.1%) touched credit or usage billing across the companies Pulse tracks over the last 180 days. The sharper read: this is a per-model credit change, not a plan-price change — Pika lowered one model's credit cost and let that ripple into how far a top-up dollar goes, a move usage-based AI products can make without touching a subscription price. For a product leader watching the seat-to-usage shift, Pika's frequent credit-table changes — rather than headline plan-price moves — are the more useful signal to track than any single credit figure.
Common questions
How do we size this for our own usage?
Multiply your expected monthly volume on this model by its new per-image rate to get your total credit spend, then compare that against the same volume under the old rate — the gap is the saving a heavy user of this model should expect to see.
Should we expect more changes like this from Pika?
Likely — Pika has been iterating on this credit table more than most companies Pulse tracks, and its most recent moves, including the Starter and Creator credit-cap cut, have all landed inside usage terms rather than the subscription price, so that layer is worth re-checking before assuming your Pika costs are stable.
What should we watch for next on this model?
Whether Pika applies the same lower rate to its other image and video models — a cost change isolated to a single row can be an early test before a wider rollout, so it's worth checking Pika's credit table again after its next pricing-page update rather than assuming the current rate holds everywhere.

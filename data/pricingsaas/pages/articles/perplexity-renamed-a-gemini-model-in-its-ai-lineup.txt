Perplexity renamed a Gemini model in its AI lineup
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS

Perplexity renamed its Gemini 4 Hydrogen model to Gemini Neon.

What changed
Perplexity's 'Multiple AI models deliver richer answers' showcase now lists Gemini Neon in place of Gemini 4 Hydrogen, per the pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 19, 2026. The entry carries a 'New' badge, the same treatment Perplexity gave Gemini 4 Hydrogen when it first appeared there on Nov 14, 2026. No prices, plans or other models in the lineup changed.
Why it matters
This is a label change, not a pricing or packaging move: Gemini Neon replaces Gemini 4 Hydrogen in the same showcase slot, with the same 'New' badge treatment and no change to Perplexity's plans, prices or credits. For a product leader, the interesting signal isn't this one swap — it's the pace: Perplexity keeps swapping models in this lineup in quick succession, which says more about how fast the underlying AI providers are shipping new models than about anything Perplexity itself decided to charge for.
If your own product bundles third-party models the way Perplexity's showcase does, this is a preview of the maintenance burden: model names and versions will keep turning over faster than your own pricing or packaging cycle, so the page copy needs a process that doesn't require a full repricing review every time a vendor renames something.
In context
Pulse has tracked 13 real pricing-page changes from Perplexity since Jul 6, 2026, and 12 of them are this same kind of model-lineup swap — this one came one day after Perplexity upgraded its included model to GPT-6.2 Sol on Nov 18, 2026, which itself followed swapping in Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Nov 14, 2026. The 'Multiple AI models deliver richer answers' showcase has existed on Perplexity's page since at least Sep 23, 2026, and its models have turned over on average every 11 days since Pulse began tracking Perplexity on Jul 6, 2026, though this past week ran faster. Pulse tracked 368 pricing changes it classified as feature changes across 298 companies in the last 180 days, 10.8% of the 3,406 changes it tracked in that window, and Perplexity itself is one of the most frequent movers in that exact pattern. The sharper read for a product leader: a 'New' badge or model swap in a showcase like this is routine maintenance, not a pricing signal — worth tracking for competitive awareness, not for budget planning.
Common questions
Is Gemini Neon a new Google model, or just a renamed Gemini 4 Hydrogen?
Perplexity's own page calls it a rename, but there's no independent confirmation of what 'Gemini Neon' maps to on Google's side — treat the capability as unverified until Google documents it elsewhere.
What should we actually track here?
Not the swap itself, but whether the badge or lineup slot stays this volatile — Perplexity has reused the 'New' badge across multiple different models in rapid succession recently, which says more about Perplexity's badge habit than about any one model's merit.

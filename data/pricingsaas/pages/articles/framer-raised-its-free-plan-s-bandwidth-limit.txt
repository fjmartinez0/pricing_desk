Framer raised its Free plan's bandwidth limit
By Rob Litterst, Co-founder, PricingSaaS
Published Nov 24, 2026
Framer raised its Free plan's monthly bandwidth limit from 1 GB to 5 GB.

What changed
Framer raised the monthly bandwidth cap on its Free plan fivefold, from 1 GB to 5 GB, per the pricing page Pulse saved on Nov 24, 2026. Nothing else on the plan's card changed — it stays free, with the same AI credit allotment, Framer domain, design pages and MCP server access it had the day before.
Pulse has tracked Framer's pricing page since Mar 23, 2026 across 35 comparisons, logging 17 real changes, 8 of them in the last 90 days. This is the company's second Free-plan move in 8 days: on Nov 16, 2026, Pulse recorded Framer making the plan's 500 AI credits renew monthly instead of expiring as a one-time trial.
Why it matters
For a product leader, this is a cheap lever: bandwidth is usually one of the lower-cost limits on a free tier, so loosening it buys goodwill without touching what Framer monetizes. It is also Framer's second Free-plan loosening in 8 days: on Nov 16, 2026, Pulse recorded Framer making the plan's 500 AI credits renew monthly instead of a one-time trial, after tightening those credits over the summer.
For your own packaging, the lesson is which limits to spend goodwill on: raise the ones that cost little per user, and meter carefully the ones with real marginal cost. Watch whether Framer's paid Basic, Pro and Enterprise tiers get a matching bandwidth bump, or whether the extra headroom stays a Free-plan acquisition play.
In context
Pulse counts 4 changes to Framer's Free plan this year, including this one, among the 17 real changes Pulse has tracked on Framer since Mar 23, 2026. It follows a Nov 16, 2026 move that turned the Free plan's AI credits into a monthly allowance instead of a one-time trial, partly reversing an August shift away from recurring credits. The pattern runs wider than one company: Pulse tracked 237 free-plan changes across 182 companies in the last 180 days, 6.1% of everything it logged in that window, with peers like Tango and Dinero also loosening free-tier limits this week. The sharper read: a bandwidth bump costs Framer little, and together with the Nov 16 credit change it makes the Free plan more generous on two fronts in the same month, after a summer of tightening its AI credits.
Common questions
What's a reasonable way to size a similar free-tier cap?
Multiply your current limit by a similar few-times factor, then check it against your real marginal cost per user — cheap-to-serve limits like storage or bandwidth can be loosened aggressively, but metered resources like AI compute usually can't.
What should I check before assuming this sticks around?
Check Framer's current pricing page directly — a saved snapshot is only a point in time, and free-tier limits are exactly the kind of lever companies revisit quickly, as Framer itself recently did with its AI credits.
How does this compare with what other companies are doing to their free tiers right now?
Peers like Tango and Dinero also loosened free-tier limits this week, suggesting this is a market-wide reflex right now rather than a one-off for Framer — though the record doesn't show whether any of them are reacting to each other or moving independently.

What happened
Between the Oct 19, 2026 and Oct 26, 2026 captures of Exa's pricing page, Pulse recorded 3 changes: With the Exa 3.0 launch, Exa removed the $15/1k Deep-Reasoning Search tier (Deep Search now a flat $12/1k), renamed Instant Search to Lite Search and Fast/Auto to Auto Search at unchanged prices, and raised the Search latency floor from 180ms to 450ms. In detail: Search tiers renamed: Instant Search → Lite Search ($4/1k unchanged) and Fast / Auto Search → Auto Search ($7/1k unchanged); Search endpoint configurable latency range changed from 180ms-1s to 450ms-1s; Deep-Reasoning Search tier ($15/1k requests) removed from endpoint pricing; Deep Search card now $12/1k requests (was $12–15/1k). Pulse files it as a packaging change (plan renamed, feature change, plan removed) with a downward direction. The current page lists Search at $7 per 1k requests, up to 10 results + $1 per 1k page AI summaries, Agent at $0.01 per search tool call + $0.1 per request (Medium effort), Contents at $1 per 1k pages, per content type + $1 per 1k page AI summaries, Deep Search at $12 per 1k requests, up to 10 results + $1 per 1k requests, additional result above 10 and Deep-Reasoning Search at $1 per 1k page AI summaries + $1 per 1k requests, additional result above 10.
It lands 21 days after Exa's previous change, "Exa launches $4/1k Instant Search, cutting Search entry price from $7" (Oct 5, 2026). Before that Pulse recorded "Exa: Free plan first-month credits cut from $30 to $20" (Sep 28, 2026), "Exa boosts Starter agent concurrency 3→50 and doubles QPS limits" (Sep 7, 2026) and "Exa: Answer endpoint overage + AI-summary fees removed" (Aug 3, 2026). Exa has been tracked since 2024-11-17.
In the same window You.com and Google also changed their AI & Machine Learning pricing pages. Those moves are listed further down, and the Pulse Agent can compare them side by side.

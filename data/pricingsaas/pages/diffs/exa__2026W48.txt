What happened
Between the Nov 16, 2026 and Nov 23, 2026 captures of Exa's pricing page, Pulse recorded 3 changes: Websets added as a priced product: $0.10/ACU + $5/1k searches, usage draws from Exa credits; legacy Starter/Pro plans keep pricing until switch; Exa Startup Program added: eligible startups get up to $15,000 in Exa credits, unlocked 1:1 as they grow; Search API configurable latency improved from 300ms-1s to 200ms-1s. Pulse files it as a pricing change (new add-on, feature change, discount added) with a neutral direction. The current page lists Search at $7 per 1k requests, up to 10 results + $1 per 1k page AI summaries, Agent at $0.01 per search tool call + $0.10 per request (Medium effort), Contents at $1 per 1k pages, per content type + $1 per 1k page AI summaries, Deep Search at $12 per 1k requests, up to 10 results + $1 per 1k requests, additional result above 10 and Monitors at $1 per 1k requests, additional result above 10 + $1 per 1k page AI summaries.
It lands 7 days after Exa's previous change, "Exa speeds up Search with configurable latency now as low as 300ms, from 450ms" (Nov 15, 2026). Before that Pulse recorded "Exa slashes Monitors API price 87% to $2, adds $12K/yr Enterprise tier" (Nov 8, 2026), "Exa drops Deep-Reasoning Search tier and renames Instant to Lite Search" (Oct 26, 2026) and "Exa launches $4/1k Instant Search, cutting Search entry price from $7" (Oct 4, 2026). Exa has been tracked since 2024-11-17.
In the same window You.com and Google also changed their AI & Machine Learning pricing pages. Those moves are listed further down, and the Pulse Agent can compare them side by side.

Frequently Asked Questions
What makes the Enterprise plan different from the Pro plan?
Our Enterprise plan allows for more customizability to your limits and additional benefits for high resource needs.
In contrast to our Pro plans, which provide shared public build environments exclusively, our Enterprise plans offer enhanced flexibility by providing fully managed options for both public and dedicated environments. This empowers you with increased security and control over your build environment. Additionally, you can self-host your build infrastructure through Bitrise on AWS or by utilizing Bitrise Agent for on-premise setups.
Enterprise also offers the best and most performant compute options as well as additional features for a more robust governance of your configuration and team.
What's the difference between Starter and Pro?
With the Starter plan, you can get your new pipeline up and running with unlimited build minutes. The Starter plan allows you to run up to four concurrent builds – ideal for individual developers and small teams just getting started with Bitrise.
As your builds and teams grow, our Pro plan gives you the opportunity to optimize the efficiency of your builds. With flexible tiers tailored to your specific needs, you can easily balance build throughput and cost with increased compute performance out of the box.
How do I upgrade to Enterprise once I’ve signed up?
Talk to us. The team will make sure the plan fits your exact needs.
What is a build?
Build means a workflow run triggered either as standalone or as part of a pipeline.
How is builds per month calculated?
For Bitrise Pro and Enterprise plans, our pricing is largely based on the number of monthly builds run on Bitrise Platform. So, you should look at your typical monthly throughput in terms of executed workflows, both successful and not, and use that as a starting point when figuring out the appropriate Tier.
How much does a build cost?
Build cost is calculated based on your pricing plan.

What is Zero Margin Infrastructure?
Bitrise’s main goal is to make our Customers’ mobile CI/CD process as efficient and productive as possible. Infrastructure resources play a big part in this. As such, we strive to offer the most performant machines to our customers, and we strive to do it at passthrough cost, without any profit-generating margin. This should allow our customers to focus on running value-add builds, and not counting credits or minutes.
What happens if I want to use more than my monthly limit?
Within the Pro plan, if you consumed the max number of builds or minutes within your tier, you can use the Pay-As-You-Go option. If you pay annually, you can also consider buying an additional 4 months' of resources through Extra Build Capacity (see FAQ below).
How does Pay-As-You-Go work?
Pay-As-You-Go (PAYG) allows all Pro Plan users to run additional builds and compute minutes even after you've reached your plan’s monthly limit. Here's how it works:
PAYG gives you access to more builds, up to an additional 50% of your pre-paid limit.
These additional builds are charged at a premium PAYG rate.
Compute minutes are unlimited under PAYG after you have used your plan minutes, but will be charged at a premium PAYG rate.
PAYG build minutes are tracked separately for macOS and Linux. Your usage on each platform is metered and charged independently.
PAYG charges are billed separately at the end of the month, even for annual subscriptions.
If your PAYG charges are less than $5, you won’t be billed.
Once you reach your PAYG build limit , you won’t be able to start new builds until your next billing cycle begins.
If you consistently rely on PAYG, upgrading your plan can reduce costs and prevent build interruptions.
How does Extra Build Capacity (EBC) work?
What is Extra Build Capacity?
Extra Build Capacity is an exclusive feature for annual Pro Plan users that lets you pre-purchase additional resources to use flexibly throughout the year.
How does it work?
You can buy up to 4 months' worth of extra builds and minutes to use anytime within your billing cycle.
Extra Build Capacity includes separate minute pools for macOS and Linux. Each platform’s minutes are allocated and tracked independently.
These extra resources allow you to handle workload spikes without upgrading your plan.
Extra Build Capacity can be purchased when subscribing or at any time during your annual cycle.
Any unused extra capacity expires at the end of your billing cycle and does not roll over.
This is a one-time purchase —it does not renew automatically, so you'll need to buy more in future cycles if needed.
Why use Extra Build Capacity?
If you have seasonal workload fluctuations, or you anticipate a surge in your usage, Extra Build Capacity ensures your team has the resources it needs without committing to a higher plan year-round.
Why is scaling with builds better than with compute time?
Total compute time is a function of number of builds * builds’ duration. However, there is a big difference between a large number of short builds and a small number of long builds. And the difference is in the value realized: shorter feedback loop, faster iterations, no context switching. Through our pricing model, Bitrise is aligning with your goals: pay for value, not hardware consumption. Build more, build faster, build smarter.
What if I want to customize my plan?
In order to create a pricing plan tailored for your needs, you can reach out to our sales team. You can fill in the form here and we will create a unique pricing solution that fits your needs.
What is a Build timeout?
The Build timeout is the maximum amount of time a workflow is able to run. When your build hits the build timeout limitation, your build will be stopped. On our Free plan, your build times out at 90 minutes, while on Starter and Pro, it can run up to 210 minutes. On Enterprise, build duration is flexible, up to a maximum of 4 hours.

Customers receiving Priority support will gain access to a customer success manager to help you achieve your most pressing goals on Bitrise.
Premium support also includes: a technical account manager, ticket SLAs, a dedicated Slack channel, and impact sessions, where we give guidance driving performance improvements.

Our Hobby tier is free. No strings attached, no credit card needed. We give you 1 private app, 5 concurrencies with 90 mins/build timeout and 300 credits per month. Try Bitrise, it’s on us.

Where can I track the number of builds and minutes consumed?
You can check your monthly cycle usage on your Workspace’s Plan & billing page. You can also check your number of builds and build minutes consumed on Insights and see historical trends. Navigate to the “Insights” tab, and find “Utilization” on the left navigation bar. If you run out of resources, you can either request a trial extension or just get going with a paid plan.
Can I activate a paid plan even before my trial ends?
Yes, you can activate a paid plan anytime during your 30-day trial. Just click on “Select plan” from the top banner whenever you are ready. Alternatively, you can go to the “Plan & Billing” section of your “Workspace Settings” to view available plans.
What happens when my trial ends?
You will be able to see the trial end date in your welcome email, on Bitrise as a banner, or even in your Workspace Settings. You can also continue using Bitrise for free, you will be able to choose our "Hobby" plan at the end of the trial where you will still be able to access the basic capabilities for free, with a credit allowance of 300 per month.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is an invocation?
An invocation is a Gradle or Bazel command (e.g. gradle test, bazel build) that runs with Bitrise Build Cache enabled. Only invocations that download data from the cache count toward your limit. Invocations that don’t result in a cache download are free.
What is a seat?
A seat represents one developer using Bitrise Build Cache and is a key component of Enterprise plans. It helps estimate the expected number of cache invocations your team will require. Each seat on an Enterprise plan includes 200 monthly invocations.
‍
To estimate how many seats your team needs, begin by counting the number of engineers actively contributing to projects using Build Cache. Keep in mind that actual usage can vary based on several factors. The most accurate way to determine your needs is by starting a free 30-day trial, which provides full visibility into your team’s real invocation usage.
Why is Enterprise pricing based on seats rather than invocations?
Seat-based pricing makes cost estimation easier, as tracking invocations can be unpredictable. Each seat includes a set number of invocations, simplifying budgeting.
Is there a minimum number of seats required?
For Enterprise plans, a minimum of 10 seats is required. If you need fewer, the Pro plan may be a better fit.
What is build cache storage?
Build Cache stores previous build and test outputs to speed up future builds. Storage needs depend on your project size—larger projects require more storage to maintain optimal performance. Our default storage package is calculated to accommodate most use cases.
What build tools are supported?
Bitrise Build Cache currently supports caching for Bazel, Gradle, and Xcode.
What CI tools does Bitrise Build Cache integrate with?
Bitrise Build Cache offers native integration with Bitrise CI, allowing you to get set up in just two minutes. It also supports other CI platforms, including CircleCI, GitHub Actions, GitLab CI, and Jenkins.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is a MAU?
Monthly active user is a unique end user who downloads one or more CodePush updates within a single monthly billing cycle. Multiple downloads by the same person in that month count as a single MAU.
What is Data Transfer Volume?
Data transfer volume is the total size of files delivered by CodePush updates to your users measured in GiB.
How much storage do I have for CodePush artifacts?
The total amount of space depends on your plan. Free tiers have 5 GiB, CodePush Pro plans have 25 GiB, and Enterprise plans start at 50GiB.
Do I need to be a Bitrise CI user to use Bitrise CodePush?
No. A Bitrise CI subscription is not required for any CodePush plan. CodePush Pro and Enterprise can both be purchased without a paid Bitrise CI plan, and the free tier is open to everyone. CodePush works alongside any CI provider.
Which features are included on the free tier?
All of them. Delta updates, code signing, staged rollouts, one-click rollback, mandatory updates, and the Bitrise CI Workflow Step are available on every plan including Basic. Paid tiers give you higher MAU, data transfer, and storage limits, not extra capabilities. See a full list of Bitrise CodePush features here.

What is Bitrise Build Hub?
Build Hub runs your GitHub Actions workflows on fully managed build infrastructure from Bitrise. You keep your existing GitHub Actions workflows and get dedicated access to powerful macOS and Linux runners, without managing your own hardware.
What Plans are available?
Three: Basic, Pro, and Enterprise.
Basic starts at $13.50/month (or $15/month if you're billed monthly instead of annually). You get access to M4 Large(5 vCPU, 14 GB RAM) and up to Linux Large (8 vCPU, 32 GB RAM) machine types with the four Basic Tiers available.
Pro starts at $189/month (or $210/month billed monthly). You get access to M4 Large(5 vCPU, 14 GB RAM) and M4 X Large(10 vCPU, 28 GB RAM) macOS machines, along with Linux X Large (16 vCPU, 64 GB RAM) machine types with the four Pro Tiers available.
Enterprise is a custom Plan, providing access to M4 Pro 192 vCPU Linux machine types. So talk to us for a quote.
What's included in the free trial?

How does the concurrency limit work?
Each Tier has a macOS and a Linux concurrency limit. On both platforms machines will start to run your Github Actions job if the started machine doesn’t exceed the concurrency limit.
‍Example 1:
If you are on Pro Tier 1 with existing machines tying down 35 vCPU macOS already, then an M4 Large (5 vCPU, 14 GB RAM) machine can spin up to serve your newly triggered Github Actions jobs, to reach your 40 vCPU macOS concurrency limit. On the other hand an M4 X Large (10 vCPU, 28 GB RAM) won’t be able to spin up, since it’s exceeding your concurrency limit.
‍Example 2:
You are on Pro Tier 1 and running already macOS machines and consuming all your macOS concurrency limit. Given that the machine concurrency limit is platform specific, your Linux Github Actions jobs will still spin up machines to serve them, if it fits within your Linux concurrency limit, despite you already reaching your macOS concurrency limit.
How does the monthly compute budget work?
Each Tier Plan includes one shared compute budget that covers both macOS and Linux builds. macOS and Linux machines draw from the same pool, but each uses it up at a different rate, so how many minutes you actually get depends on how you split your usage between the two.
‍Example 1:
On Basic Tier 4 you get a monthly budget of $180 regardless of monthly or annual billing(which comes with 10% discount). The $180 monthly budget gets you 6000 M4 Large minutes if you use macOS exclusively, given that the M4 Large machine has a $0.03 per minute cost.
If you use it exclusively on Linux Medium machines, then your Github Actions jobs can run a total of 14,400 minutes per month, thanks to the $0.0125 price per minute of the machine type.
Of course you can use both platforms at the same time time. Check out the Price / min rate under the “machine rates and overages” under the “Your monthly budget flex between Mac and Linux” section.
What happens if my included compute budget runs out?
Pay-As-You-Go is off by default, meaning your GitHub Actions jobs simply won’t run on Build Hub Runners if your monthly budget is used up.
You can choose to turn on Pay-As-You-Go mode to ensure your GitHub Actions jobs will continue to run on Build Hub after your monthly budget is consumed. The Pay-As-You-Go per-minute rate is 2x the standard rate for your machine type, so you might consider switching to a higher Build Hub Tier if you run out of your monthly budget regularly.
You stay in control of spend. Set a Pay-As-You-Go cap from $5 up to unlimited, and Bitrise won’t start Runners for your GitHub Actions jobs after you reach the extra cost you are comfortable with spending.
What do I get with Enterprise?
Enterprise is for teams that need the strongest machines to run at unparalleled scale. The Bitrise Build Hub Enterprise offering includes the full machine range (Apple silicon up to M4 Pro, Linux up to 96 vCPU).
Additionally the All-You-Can-Eat pricing model, provides unlimited Github Action jobs minutes within your dedicated vCPU concurrency.
Can I switch Plans later?
Yes. You can start with Basic Tier 1 and upgrade to any higher Tier at any time as your team's needs grow, or move to Enterprise by talking to our team.
